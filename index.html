Reading the taxonomy

Critic / Validator is the most widely adopted archetype (57 of 86) — students consistently designed something to check the model's own output.

Domain-specific agents outnumber every functional archetype. Names like Pantry Manager Cycle Analysis Recovery Protocol Zoning show students reaching for their problem's vocabulary rather than a generic pattern.

Orchestrator / Manager appears in only 18 designs. Most students wired agents directly to each other and left coordination implicit.

What that means

The sequential pipeline is the class's default mental model: input enters, each agent transforms it, output leaves. It is the easiest to reason about and the easiest to build in Opal or n8n.

Parallel execution — the pattern that actually buys agentic systems their speed — shows up in 16 designs. Genuine concurrency was the least-internalized idea of the term.

What they planned for — and what they missed

Design concerns raised anywhere in a student's writing. The top of this list is genuinely encouraging: most of the class thought about failure before it happened. The bottom is where next term's syllabus has work to do.

  • Error handling / fallback70
  • Privacy & PII69
  • Hallucination guardrails61
  • Cost / latency58
  • Feedback loop57
  • Evaluation metrics40
  • Memory / state32
  • Bias & fairness23
  • Human-in-the-loop17
  • RAG / vector search8

The gap: only 17 of 86 students designed an explicit human checkpoint — a place where a person approves an action before the system takes it. Students readily added a critic agent to review the model, but rarely a human to review the agent. For systems that book appointments, submit job applications, or offer medical guidance, that is the consequential omission.

The Week 1 slide that propagated

The planner–executor–critic pattern was introduced once, in the Week 1 deck. Counting how many students later named an agent after each part:

Adoption of the taught pattern
  • Planner agent51
  • Critic agent51
  • Executor agent13
  • All three named9
Partial adoption

Planner and critic transferred cleanly — 51 and 51 students respectively. Both are easy to describe and easy to justify in a proposal.

The executor did not: 13 students named one, and only 9 used the full triad. The class absorbed the parts of the pattern that think and skipped the part that acts — the same asymmetry visible in the wiring and human-in-the-loop numbers.
